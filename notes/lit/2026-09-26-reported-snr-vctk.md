# Reported SNR on VCTK: audio super-resolution / bandwidth extension

Every number below was read from the paper's own text (PDF text pulled with pypdf, or the rendered page image for HiFi-GAN+). None were recalled from memory. "Source" gives the arXiv id and table. One exception: the Wang & Wang TASLP 2021 rows (marked `*`). That paper is not on arXiv, and its PMC page sits behind a bot check, so those rows came through a WebFetch transcription. I fetched them twice and both reads agreed. Check them against the PDF before you rely on them.

Role legend:
- **INTERP (...)**: the paper's own input, linear, spline or sinc row, measured on the paper's own protocol. These are the rows that stand in for the ceiling.
- own: the authors' model.
- own-ablation: a variant of the authors' model.
- baseline-rerun: a baseline the authors trained or evaluated themselves.
- baseline-copied: a number copied from another paper, so it was measured on that other paper's protocol.

"not stated" means I searched the paper for it and did not find it. It does not mean the answer is "no".

A derived identity that helps map SI-SNR to SNR: take a perfect low band with an empty high band (the output is exactly the target's low band, orthogonal to the missing band). For that signal, linear SNR = linear SI-SNR + 1. So SNR_dB = 10 log10(10^(SI/10) + 1). The two differ by less than 0.05 dB above 20 dB. Bridge-SR's Input SI-SNR (20.05 at 8k) therefore implies an input SNR of about 20.09 dB. NU-Wave 2 reports 19.0 dB. Both papers claim the same protocol, and the numbers still differ by about 1 dB.

## E. Discrepancies and caveats that move SNR

1. **NU-Wave's "Linear" row is not a ceiling proxy.** It reports 11.1 dB at 24k->48k on the multi-speaker split. NU-Wave 2's "Input" row, on the same last-8-speaker split, is 29.4 dB. The two papers use different filters (STFT zeroing vs Chebyshev), and NU-Wave's linear interpolation of the subsampled signal is far from ideal band-limited reconstruction. NU-Wave's +1.9 to +3.3 dB over "Linear" is therefore a gain over a weak baseline, not over the ceiling.
2. **Spline rows at 16 kHz differ between papers that describe the same protocol.** On Multi r=2, Kuleshov reports spline 19.7 and TFiLM reports 18.0. On Multi r=4 they report 13.0 and 13.2. TFiLM's re-run of the Kuleshov CNN gives 18.1 (Multi r=2), while Kuleshov reports 20.7 for that model. Cubic spline on a Chebyshev-filtered, subsampled signal is also not a perfect low-band reconstruction, so these rows sit below the true ceiling.
3. **Some cross-paper numbers exceed NU-Wave 2's input ceiling, but on different protocols.** WSRGlow reports 30.84 (24k) and LISA 30.7 (24k), against NU-Wave 2's input 29.4. LISA also reports 24.16 at 12k (vs 22.1) and 22.17 at 8k (vs 19.0). WSRGlow uses a random utterance split with speakers seen in training, and librosa resampling. LISA uses sinc downsampling and a first-99-speakers split. Neither reports an input row, so these rows cannot be tested against a ceiling without recomputing one on each protocol. The spread is wide even for one model: WSRGlow at 24k->48k is 30.84 in its own paper, 25.29 in LISA and 26.6 in NU-Wave 2.
4. **mdctGAN Table 1 mixes protocols.** AudioUNet and MUGAN are copied from WSRGlow (seen speakers, librosa). WSRGlow and NU-Wave 2 are copied from NU-Wave 2 (Chebyshev, unseen speakers). mdctGAN's own rows use torchaudio resample on an unstated split. It has no input row.
5. **The same checkpoint scores differently in different papers.** FastWave runs the official NU-Wave 2 checkpoint (epoch 629) and gets 18.43 / 20.95 / 23.31 / 27.68. NU-Wave 2 reports 18.8 / 21.6 / 24.0 / 28.4. That is 0.4-0.7 dB lower, with FastWave's filter not stated.
6. **NU-Wave 2 aggregation.** The paper does not say how SNR is aggregated (per-utterance mean vs global). I found no such sentence. The per-utterance claim may come from the code; it is not in the paper.
7. **LISA split wording.** The paper says "the first 99 speakers are used for training and the rest are served as test set", inherited from Kuleshov. It does not say whether p280/p315 are excluded or whether only mic1 is used.
8. **MU-GAN's target rate is implied, not stated.** The paper only says its datasets "are the same as those used in [22]" (Kuleshov, 16 kHz). Its multi-speaker test set is the last 10 speakers, not 8.
