# Inference-time readouts for a calibrated energy-score BWE sampler

## 1. Which statistic is optimal for which metric

**Distortion vs perception.** Blau & Michaeli define distortion E[Δ(X,X̂)] and perceptual index d(p_X, p_X̂); Theorem 2: the perception–distortion function is monotone non-increasing and convex "for any distortion measure". Theorem 3 (square error): D_max ≤ 2·D_min, attained by the posterior sampler p_{X̂|Y}=p_{X|Y}, which "achieves p_X̂ = p_X and has an MSE of 2·D_min" (orthogonality principle); MMSE = posterior mean E[X|Y=y] (https://ar5iv.labs.arxiv.org/html/1711.06077). Freirich–Michaeli–Meir give the closed form D(P) = D* + [(P* − P)_+]², P* = W₂(p_X, p_{X*}); the optimal estimator at perception P is X̂_P = (1 − P/P*)·X̂₀ + (P/P*)·X*, a linear interpolation between the MMSE estimate X* and its optimal-transport image X̂₀ = T(X*); posterior sampling sits at (P=0, D=2D*) and "can be improved upon in terms of MSE without any sacrifice in perceptual quality" (https://ar5iv.labs.arxiv.org/html/2107.02555). PMRF implements T on the posterior mean and reports CelebA-Test FID 37.46 / PSNR 26.37 vs DifFace FID 38.43 / PSNR 24.80 (https://arxiv.org/html/2410.00418). Ohayon et al. 2023 prove perfect perceptual quality + consistency ⟺ posterior sampler, and that deterministic models with high perceptual quality "must adopt an erratic output behavior" (https://ar5iv.labs.arxiv.org/html/2211.08944).

**Averaging samples = MMSE.** PSCGAN (FFHQ, σ=25): single draw 29.19 dB, average of N=64 draws 31.46 dB, MSE-trained net 31.83 dB, DnCNN 31.77 dB; "PSCGAN-A approaches Ours-MSE as N increases" (https://ar5iv.labs.arxiv.org/html/2103.04192). The 2.3 dB gain is close to the 3 dB (=2×) predicted by Theorem 3.

**Non-MSE metrics elicit different functionals.** Gneiting: a scoring function S is consistent for functional T if E_F[S(t,Y)] ≤ E_F[S(x,Y)] for all t∈T(F); squared error → mean, absolute error → "any median of the predictive distribution", pinball → quantile (https://ar5iv.labs.arxiv.org/html/0912.0902). Consequences for your metrics:
- **LSD** (per frame: sqrt(mean_k (log10 P̂_k − log10 P_k)²)) is squared error in the log-power domain, so its Bayes readout is the conditional mean of log-power, i.e. exp of the mean of log|X_m|² over draws (the geometric-mean spectrogram), *not* the log of the mean waveform. This is exactly Ephraim–Malah's LSA principle: the estimator "minimizes the mean squared error between the LSAs of the clean and estimated signals", gain G_LSA = ξ/(1+ξ)·exp(½∫_v^∞ e^{−t}/t dt) (secondary statements only: https://www.researchgate.net/publication/3177752 and http://www.seas.ucla.edu/spapl/paper/borgstrom_icassp_11.pdf; I could not open the 1985 paper itself). Loizou 2005 generalises to weighted-Euclidean and log-MMSE magnitude estimators (https://ieeexplore.ieee.org/document/1495469/).
- **L1 waveform** → per-sample median of draws.
- **ViSQOL-audio**: NSIM = [(2μ_rμ_d + C₁)/(μ_r² + μ_d² + C₁)]·[(σ_rd + C₃)/(σ_rσ_d + C₃)] over a 3×3 Gaussian window on the gammatone neurogram, averaged over time–frequency (https://arxiv.org/html/2506.12705, citing Hines & Harte 2012); ViSQOL v3 uses 32 ERB gammatone bands, ~0.5 s patches, SVR mapping to MOS (https://ar5iv.labs.arxiv.org/html/2004.09584). The luminance term rewards correct band *means* (envelope); the structure term rewards local *correlation* of the 3×3 patch. No paper I found derives an optimal ensemble readout for NSIM. Reasoning from the formula: a band above 6 kHz that is incoherent with the input has σ_rd ≈ 0 for any draw; the structure term is then near C₃/(σ_rσ_d + C₃), which *rises* when σ_d shrinks toward the envelope. So an envelope-only high band should not lose on structure and gains on luminance.
- **The waveform ensemble mean is the wrong readout for LSD**: for the incoherent band, averaging M independent draws divides band power by ≈M (−10·log10 M dB), so LSD rises with M even as SNR improves — consistent with your "ensemble mean beats deterministic on SNR" finding.

## 2. Ensemble size laws and diagnostics

From Theorem 3, each draw has E‖X_m − X*‖² = D*, so the mean of M draws has MSE_M = D*(1 + 1/M): the (M+1)/M law. Fortin et al. 2014: Skill ≈ sqrt((M+1)/M)·Spread for a reliable ensemble, spread = sqrt of mean ensemble variance, "not the average of member standard deviations" (abstract paywalled; statement via https://journals.ametsoc.org/view/journals/hydr/15/4/jhm-d-14-0008_1.xml search summary and https://arxiv.org/html/2408.03100v3, which defines spread as "the square root of the global-mean ensemble variance"). Ensemble CRPS estimator CRPS_M = (1/M)Σ|x_j − y| − (1/2M²)ΣΣ|x_j − x_k|; fair CRPS replaces 2M² by 2M(M−1) (https://arxiv.org/html/2412.15832v1, https://arxiv.org/html/2603.01949). Taking expectations for a reliable exchangeable ensemble gives E[CRPS_M] = CRPS_∞·(1 + 1/M) — my derivation; Leutbecher 2019 (https://rmets.onlinelibrary.wiley.com/doi/10.1002/qj.3387) was paywalled, so I could not verify his exact form. Your two-draw loss ½[d(y,y₁)+d(y,y₂)] − ½d(y₁,y₂) is the fair CRPS/energy score at M=2. Caveat: for the fair score, "the optimal value for c is undefined for N<3" (spread parameter degenerate) and fair-score-trained networks tend to over-dispersion (spread/RMSE ≈ 1.20 at N=9) while unfair-score training is under-dispersive (https://arxiv.org/html/2602.15830); AIFS-CRPS uses afCRPS_α = α·fCRPS + (1−α)·CRPS, α=0.95, to avoid a degeneracy "where all members apart from one have the same value as the verifying observation" (https://arxiv.org/html/2412.15832v1). Diagnostics: U-shaped rank histogram = under-dispersion; spread/skill ≠ 1 = mis-calibrated variance (ArchesWeatherGen DDPM baseline: spread-skill 0.7 at 24 h, https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC13101865/).

## 3. Noise temperature and per-band shaping after training

- Glow: sample p_{θ,T} ∝ p_θ^{T²}, implemented by "multiplying the standard deviation of p_θ(z) by a factor of T"; T=0.7 "a sweet spot for diversity and quality" (https://ar5iv.labs.arxiv.org/html/1807.03039). WaveGlow: "During training we used σ=0.5, and during inference we sampled z from a Gaussian with standard deviation 0.6", which "resulted in slightly higher quality audio" (https://ar5iv.labs.arxiv.org/html/1811.00002). BigGAN truncation: resample |z| above a threshold; IS rises to 166.5 while FID worsens to 25 vs best FID 7.7 at IS 126.5 (https://ar5iv.labs.arxiv.org/html/1809.11096). All three trade diversity for fidelity — the opposite direction from what a calibrated sampler needs.
- CRPS/energy-trained ensembles: ArchesWeatherGen scales the initial noise by 1.05 ("roughly the percentage of overfitting"), raising the 24 h spread-skill from 0.9 to ≈0.96, with "no effect on RMSE, CRPS or BrierScore"; per-variable scaling suggested but not tested (https://arxiv.org/html/2412.12971). FMAP notes energy-score/flow postprocessors are "underdispersive at smaller lead times, despite having better marginal metric scores" and defers noise scaling to future work (https://arxiv.org/html/2504.03463).
- Per-frequency noise: FlowDec sets σ_y(f) = (1/3)·sqrt(Q(|X* − Y|², 0.997)) per STFT bin (768-point curve, Gaussian-smoothed, bandwidth 3) because "a single σ_y value can cause excessive smoothing when added Gaussian noise dominates high-frequency regions"; frequency-dependent σ improves FAD by ≈0.28–0.30 vs global σ_y=0.66 (https://arxiv.org/html/2503.01485). PriorGrad uses N(0, Σ_c) with per-sample variance from mel energy and loss ‖ε − ε_θ‖²_{Σ⁻¹}; LS-MAE 0.5264→0.5048, MR-STFT 1.0920→0.9976 vs DiffWave (https://ar5iv.labs.arxiv.org/html/2106.06406). SpecGrad filters the noise so "its time-varying spectral envelope becomes close to the conditioning log-mel spectrogram", with claimed high-band improvements (https://arxiv.org/abs/2203.16749). Not verified: any paper tuning a *per-band* temperature post hoc for an energy-score model.

Important for you: temperature moves *spread*, not *bias*. A −7.9 dB high-band energy deficit at the CRPS-optimal τ=1 is a bias in a nonlinear functional (energy), which τ cannot repair without moving the CRPS off its minimum.

## 4. Sample selection

MBR/best-of-N in audio: ArtiFree draws S=3–10 diffusion outputs, builds a wav2vec frame-embedding similarity matrix and picks the medoid ("ensemble centrality"); low-SNR WER 0.438 (single) → 0.373 (S=10 centrality) → 0.279 with an oracle clean-reference selector; PESQ drop ≈0.1 when steps are cut (https://arxiv.org/html/2509.19495). Best-of-N TTS with ASR verifiers: WER −8.7 % (best verifier), 1.61 % at N=10, but "verifier rankings are systematically evaluator-dependent"; calibration is not discussed (https://arxiv.org/html/2607.08256v1). MBR in ASR/MT selects the hypothesis with highest expected utility against the other samples; gains saturate at 4–8 samples (https://arxiv.org/html/2510.19471v1). Landesberg: with a miscalibrated judge the best-of-N "calibration gap is non-decreasing as N increases" (https://arxiv.org/pdf/2603.12520). By Gneiting's consistency argument the medoid under d is a point estimate of a functional, not a draw from p(x|y); its outputs form a sharper-than-posterior distribution and a hump-shaped PIT. No audio paper measures this; treat as prediction.

## 5. Hybrid envelope + stochastic fine structure

Jax (2004): "original spectral envelope and modified excitation in extended frequency bands result in perceived quality only slightly inferior to original wideband speech" (cited in https://ietresearch.onlinelibrary.wiley.com/doi/full/10.1049/iet-spr.2015.0375; page 403 for me, so the quote is from a search summary). LPCNet adds training noise "in the μ-law domain" and at synthesis sharpens by P′(e_t) = R(max[R(P(e_t)^c) − T, 0]), c = 1 + max(0, 1.5·g_p − 0.5), T=0.002 (https://ar5iv.labs.arxiv.org/html/1810.11846). DSP-informed BWE: neural exciter + linear time-varying filter from a predicted acoustic-feature envelope; the loss "implicitly influences the exciter to produce flat spectra in the upper frequency band"; MUSHRA 78.42 vs 75.93 (https://arxiv.org/html/2407.15624v1). Tachibana 2018 (WaveNet): prediction noise "tend[s] to cause large spectral distortion in a high-frequency band"; perceptual-weighting noise shaping "significantly improved" quality (https://sigport.org/documents/investigation-noise-shaping-perceptual-weighting-wavenet-based-speech-generation). Gritsenko's spectral energy distance L_GED = E[2d(x,y) − d(y,y′)] with d = Σ_k Σ_t ‖s_t^k(x_i) − s_t^k(x_j)‖₁ + α_k‖log s_t^k(x_i) − log s_t^k(x_j)‖₂: removing the repulsive term cut MOS 4.03→3.00 ("metallic") (https://ar5iv.labs.arxiv.org/html/2008.01160). PNS in MPEG-4 AAC (noise-like bands replaced by noise plus an energy parameter): pages unreachable (https://publica.fraunhofer.de/entities/publication/3d808d4c-86ee-4dc4-be02-770ce02433fa), so unverified.

## 6. Calibration repair after training

Kuleshov et al.: "a simple procedure for calibrating any regression algorithm" (isotonic map on predicted CDF values); only the abstract was accessible (https://proceedings.mlr.press/v80/kuleshov18a.html). Member-by-member post-processing: x̃_i = (a + b·x̄) + γ·(x_i − x̄), γ = c + d·δ(x), δ = mean pairwise member difference, (a,b,c,d) fit by minimising CRPS; it preserves the rank/correlation structure; CRPS 0.97 vs EMOS 0.95 on wind gusts (https://ar5iv.labs.arxiv.org/html/2106.09512; theory: https://rmets.onlinelibrary.wiley.com/doi/10.1002/qj.2397, paywalled). EMOS: predictive variance linear in ensemble spread, coefficients by minimum-CRPS (https://journals.ametsoc.org/view/journals/mwre/133/5/mwr2904.1.xml). A per-band affine map on log-magnitude (a_k, γ_k) is therefore a standard, proper-score-consistent operation *if* fitted by CRPS minimisation on held-out data; a per-band scale on spread alone (γ_k) is the "evmos"/inflation special case.

## Ranked cheapest inference-time changes (M = 8 draws, batched; 88k params fits the 20 ms budget)

1. **Geometric-mean magnitude readout.** |X̂_k| = exp(mean_m log|X_{m,k}|), phase from draw 1, iSTFT. Prediction: LSD falls below the deterministic model and decreases monotonically in M following Var_log/M; the waveform mean's LSD *rises* with M by ≈10·log10 M dB of band-energy error. Ensemble untouched → CRPS/PIT unchanged by construction.
2. **Envelope-from-ensemble, fine structure from one draw.** Smooth (1) over 32 ERB bands to get an envelope E_k; output = draw 1 × E_k / env(draw 1). Prediction: ViSQOL-audio rises ≥0.3 MOS over a single draw (luminance term fixed, structure term unchanged), LSD between (1) and a single draw.
3. **Per-band MBM correction on log-magnitude** (a_k + γ_k·(x_i − x̄)) fitted by fair-CRPS minimisation on a held-out set. Prediction: high-band energy deficit −7.9 dB → within ±1 dB, CRPS strictly decreases (proper), PIT stays flat; on the +0.6 dB set the fitted a_k ≈ 0 for k > 6 kHz.
4. **MBR/medoid selection under d** (multi-scale log-STFT L1) among the 8 draws. Prediction: LSD −0.05 to −0.1 and ViSQOL +0.1–0.2 vs a random draw, saturating by N≈8 (MBR literature); the PIT of *selected* outputs becomes hump-shaped, so report calibration on the unselected ensemble.
5. **Per-band temperature τ_k** set so per-band spread/skill = sqrt(M/(M+1)) (FlowDec-style). Prediction: small CRPS gain (< 3 %), LSD neutral, energy deficit unchanged.
6. **Global τ sweep** (control). Prediction: τ<1 (WaveGlow/Glow direction) lowers high-band energy further and worsens LSD in-distribution; τ≈1.05 (ArchesWeatherGen) leaves CRPS flat and LSD flat; neither fixes the bias.
