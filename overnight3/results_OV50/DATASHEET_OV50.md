# OV50 master datasheet

Generated by `fast/datasheet.py` from `results_OV50.json`, `visqol_OV50.json`, `val_wave_OV50.json`, `val_wave_decompose_OV50.json` and the eight histories. EVAL12: n = 12 held-out utterances (p236-p238), M = 16 draws. Training: 104950 steps, batch 64, one shared batch stream, so the arms are paired step for step.

## Read this first

| trap | the rule |
|---|---|
| **LSD is not dB** | `lsd_db` omits the factor of 10. Values are decades of power; x10 for dB. Not comparable with LISA's published LSD, which uses a different transform, axis, epsilon and cap. |
| **raw vs passthrough** | Every LSD/ViSQOL figure exists both ways. The 16 Sep note reports passthrough; the EVAL12 table's `LSD` column is raw. Both are labelled here. |
| **readout** | A sampler has three outputs (one draw, `mean16`, `logmean16`), a deterministic arm has one. Compare each model at its best readout for the metric in question. |
| **`val_wave` is a proper score** | Not a reconstruction error. A sampler's value is its single-draw L1 minus half its spread. Both parts are in section 2. |
| **SNR is inert** | The floor condition emits no high band at all and outscores six of eight arms. |
| **CRPS vs a det arm** | A point forecast is a degenerate distribution and must lose a proper score. `det`/`det_paper` are the reference line on CRPS and `val_wave`, not competitors. |

**The readouts trace an energy/distortion curve and averaging moves along it monotonically.** More draws averaged means less high-band energy (deficit falls) and a better estimate of the log-magnitude conditional mean (LSD falls, then LSD's own optimum is reached). One draw holds the energy; `logmean16` holds the spectrum; `logmean4` sits between and is the best single compromise if energy matters as much as distortion. `mean16` -- averaging the WAVEFORMS rather than the log-magnitudes -- is dominated on both LSD and deficit by `logmean16` while winning SNR and PESQ, which is the clearest statement in the run that those two judges are measuring the wrong thing. A deterministic arm occupies ONE point on this curve and cannot move along it.

## 6. Perceptual -- each judge's whole range for this task

| judge | floor | ceiling | range | `naive` (no high band) sits at |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| **ViSQOL audio48k** | 1.567 | 4.725 | **3.158** | 0.3% |
| ViSQOL speech16k | 3.947 | 4.508 | **0.561** | 4.4% |
| PESQ wb | 4.285 | 4.615 | **0.330** | 27.9% |
| NSIM audio | 0.724 | 0.994 | **0.270** | 1.0% |
| NSIM speech | 0.961 | 0.995 | **0.034** | 7.3% |

Lead with **audio48k**: 32 ERB bands to 24 kHz, so it sees the whole invented band, and `naive` correctly sits at 0.3% of it. Speech mode resamples to 16 kHz and sees ~11% of the problem; passthrough alone, which touches only the baseband, moves it by 82% of its range. PESQ puts `naive` 28% of the way to perfect and ranks `det_paper` 2nd of 43 conditions. Both should be retired for 12 -> 48 kHz; NSIM is the mapping-free score and MOS-LQO saturates below 5.
