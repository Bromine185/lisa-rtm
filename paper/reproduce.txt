   One split is a lottery -- the aliased number spans an order of magnitude across the

   For an ideal brick wall the high band is exactly in ker(A) and the zero filter is optimal;
   for an 81-tap Kaiser it is optimal to within the 2.15 % that leaks.

5. reading the table honestly
   The rho = 0 row is a hard bound only for an ideal H.  The fitted anti-aliased filter
   crosses it by +0.015 utterance-dB and +0.013 pooled -- the leak of §2,
   worth three hundredths of a decibel.  Round it off and the bound holds.
   The aliased protocol's own naive baseline sits -2.55 dB lower, and the
   oracle recovers +2.47 dB of that -- about half from the baseband the fold
   corrupted, about half from the high band itself.  That is a statement about how much
   room a metric has on each protocol, not a measured information ceiling for the aliased
   one: no admissible estimator here reaches it, and on p236-p238 it lands below the
   anti-aliased bound rather than above.  What is protocol-level and assumption-free is §1.
