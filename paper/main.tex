\begin{abstract}
Generative audio bandwidth extension (BWE) models are meant to sample the missing high band, yet are
evaluated with single-reference metrics. We audit four released 12$\to$48\,kHz systems with checks used for
stochastic super-resolution of weather fields (fair CRPS, rank histograms, spread), after showing that for
incoherent high bands, waveform spread measures output level as much as diversity. FLowHigh ranks first on
LSD and ViSQOL yet is a regressor: one Euler step returns the conditional mean for any start noise.
NU-Wave~2 samples, but 8--9\,dB too quietly in the high band. AudioSR is under-diverse and biased by band.
On an 88k-parameter network, the deficit is set mainly by the loss domain and is closed by an energy score
at no inference cost.
\end{abstract}

\section{Introduction}
Many high bands $y$ are consistent with one narrow band $x$, which limits deterministic BWE approaches
\citep{jax2002,bruna2016}. For learned BWE, point estimation against a single reference waveform returns a
conditional mean, close to silence when the band is incoherent: a muffled output. Diffusion and flow models
\citep{han2022,liu2024,yun2025} are meant to avoid this by sampling $p(y\mid x)$; to our knowledge,
however, no BWE work checks whether they actually do. Instead, they are evaluated with single-reference
metrics such as SNR, which rewards an empty band when the band is incoherent \citep{su2021}. Classical BWE
already tied the high-band level to the width of this posterior \citep{nilsson2001}. Stochastic
downscaling of weather fields checks this with proper scores \citep{gneiting2007} and rank histograms
\citep{leinonen2021}. We bring those measures to BWE, with (i)~a separation of level from dispersion that
keeps them meaningful when the band is incoherent, (ii)~an audit of four released systems through one
scorer, and (iii)~a controlled study separating the loss domain from sampling.

\textbf{Setup.} VCTK~0.92, test split of FLowHigh and NU-Wave~2, with its input protocol (order-8 Chebyshev
low-pass, 12\,kHz); its eight test speakers are unseen in training by every model. Levels, LSD and ViSQOL
(v3, audio mode) use 240 utterances; calibration uses 32 utterances $\times$ 8 draws. Outputs are scaled to
the truth's energy below 5.5\,kHz. The \emph{deficit} is $10\log_{10}$ of output over true energy per third
octave above 6\,kHz, averaged over bands. CRPS is the fair ensemble estimator on high-band (HB)
log-magnitude; a one-output model scores its MAE. The harness reproduces published LSD/ViSQOL: 0.761/3.61
for FLowHigh (reported 0.75/3.61), 0.780/3.48 for AP-BWE \citep{lu2024} (0.78/3.46); NU-Wave~2 is slightly
worse, 0.989/2.57 against 0.94/2.75.

\section{The Relation Between Dispersion and Level}
Let the truth's high band have energy $E$, the output's $pE$, and let
$c=\mathrm{Re}\langle y_H,\hat y_H\rangle/E$ be the coherent fraction. With a correct low band, the squared
error is $\|y-\hat y\|^2=E\,(1+p-2c)$. An empty band costs $E$, and only $c>p/2$ beats it, so when
$c\approx0$ silence maximizes SNR. On our utterances this ceiling $C$ is 22.02\,dB at 12\,kHz, and a
reported SNR $S$ bounds the level: $p\le10^{(C-S)/10}-1+2c$. NU-Wave~2's own table ($C-S=0.5$\,dB) gives
at most $-8.5$\,dB; we measure a broadband deficit of $-8.9$\,dB ($-7.96$\,dB band-averaged,
Table~\ref{tab:released}). Every model has $c<0.02$.

For $M$ zero-mean draws independent of one another and of the truth, the gap
$G=10\log_{10}\bigl(\overline{\|y-x_i\|^2}/\|y-\bar x\|^2\bigr)$ is, in expectation,
$10\log_{10}\frac{1+p}{1+p/M}$: a function of level alone. A calibrated sampler has $p=1$ and a gap of
2.50\,dB at $M=8$, the spread--skill relation for a reliable ensemble \citep{fortin2014}. We report level
first and judge each model's gap against the independent value at its own level.

\section{Auditing Released Models}
\textbf{FLowHigh is a regressor.} It is trained from $x_0+\epsilon$ ($x_0$: input log-mel) toward
$(x_1-x_0)-(1-\sigma)\epsilon$ and also receives $x_0$ as conditioning, so at $t=0$ it can recover
$\epsilon$. The optimal field then returns $\mathrm{E}[x_1\mid x_0]+\sigma s\epsilon$ after one Euler step,
for any start-noise scale $s$; the released script also resets $s$ to $10^{-4}$. With the trained prior
restored, its draws remain within $10^{-5}$ of each other: the gap is 0.08\,dB against 1.77 for independent
draws at its level, and the truth lies below or above all eight draws in 41\% and 46\% of HB bins.

\textbf{NU-Wave~2 samples, quietly.} Its gap (0.57\,dB) is close to that of independent draws at its level
(0.63). What fails is its level: 9.0\,dB under on loud frames and 4.7\,dB over on quiet ones. Its small
spread (0.094) is a level problem, not a diversity one.

\textbf{AudioSR is under-dispersed.} Its gap (2.62\,dB) is well below that of independent draws at its
level (3.92), and its level is biased by band ($+5$\,dB at 11\,kHz, $-11$\,dB at 21\,kHz; truth above every
draw in 52\% of HB bins). AP-BWE is a point estimate by design.

\section{What Sets the Deficit}
Six arms of an 88k-parameter INR derived from LISA \citep{kim2022} are trained on identical batches for
104{,}950 steps. \texttt{det\_paper} reproduces LISA's released L1 loss; \texttt{det} adds a log-magnitude
term to the same point estimate. The remaining four are samplers, with 8 Gaussian input channels
concatenated with the signal, and 4 per output sample in the decoder-noise arm (Table~\ref{tab:arms}).
\texttt{det\_paper} leaves the band 21.8\,dB short; the log-magnitude term recovers 15.6\,dB, and replacing
the point loss by a two-draw energy score \citep{gritsenko2020,bouchacourt2016} recovers 5.1\,dB more. The
loss domain, not point estimation as such, does most of the work: FLowHigh, a conditional mean in the
log-mel domain, is not quiet. The ERB-band term lowers the band-correlation error from 0.374 to 0.289. Our
sampler is not calibrated either: the truth lies above all draws in 21\% of HB bins against an ideal 11\%,
with the lower tail at its ideal; a pure level shift would also empty the lower tail, so this is a missing
upper tail: too few loud high-band events.

\section{Conclusion}
Single-reference metrics cannot tell whether a BWE model samples, and with incoherent bands spread must be
read against level. We suggest reporting level (per band and frame loudness), diversity relative to the
model's own level, and rank tails beside LSD and ViSQOL. CRPS favours samplers by construction, so we
compare it among samplers only. A low level is a calibration failure for a sampler, not necessarily a
perceptual one \citep{nilsson2001}. Limitations: no listening test, marginal calibration only, 32
utterances, one seed, VCTK only (derivations: supplement).
