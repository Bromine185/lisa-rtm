\begin{abstract}
Generative audio bandwidth extension (BWE) models are meant to sample the missing high band, yet are
evaluated with single-reference metrics. We audit four released 12$\to$48\,kHz systems on unseen anechoic
speech with checks used for stochastic super-resolution of weather fields (fair CRPS, rank histograms,
spread), after showing that for incoherent high bands, waveform spread measures output level as much as
diversity. FLowHigh is a regressor: one Euler step returns the conditional mean for any start noise.
NU-Wave~2 samples, but 9\,dB too quietly in the high band. AudioSR leads on LSD and ViSQOL yet is
under-diverse. Every model pulls each utterance's level toward the mean. On one 88k-parameter network, the
loss domain sets the level, and an energy score fills the band at no inference cost.
\end{abstract}

\section{Introduction}
Many high bands $y$ are consistent with one narrow band $x$, which limits deterministic BWE approaches
\citep{jax2002,bruna2016}. For learned BWE, point estimation against a single reference waveform returns a
conditional mean, close to silence when the band is incoherent: a muffled output. Diffusion and flow models
\citep{han2022,liu2024,yun2025} are meant to avoid this by sampling $p(y\mid x)$; to our knowledge,
however, no BWE work checks whether they actually do. Instead, they are evaluated with single-reference
metrics such as SNR, which rewards an empty band when the band is incoherent \citep{su2021}. Classical BWE
already tied the high-band level to the width of this posterior \citep{nilsson2001}. Stochastic
downscaling of weather fields checks this with proper scores \citep{gneiting2007} and rank histograms
\citep{leinonen2021}. We bring those measures to BWE, with (i)~a separation of level from dispersion that
keeps them meaningful when the band is incoherent, (ii)~an audit of four released systems through one
scorer, and (iii)~a controlled study separating the loss domain from sampling.

\textbf{Setup.} EARS \citep{richter2024}, 48\,kHz anechoic speech, its six test speakers: 96 four-second
read utterances, 16 per speaker by a fixed rule. No audited model and not our network was trained on it.
Inputs follow FLowHigh and NU-Wave~2 (order-8 Chebyshev low-pass, 12\,kHz); every metric uses all 96
utterances $\times$ 8 draws. Outputs are scaled to the truth's energy below 5.5\,kHz. The \emph{deficit} is
$10\log_{10}$ of output over true energy per third octave above 6\,kHz, averaged over bands. CRPS is the
fair ensemble estimator on high-band (HB) log-magnitude; a one-output model scores its MAE. Brackets give
95\% intervals from 10{,}000 resamples of utterances, not of bins, which are correlated within an
utterance. On the VCTK test split the harness reproduces published LSD/ViSQOL: 0.761/3.61 for FLowHigh
(reported 0.75/3.61), 0.780/3.48 for AP-BWE \citep{lu2024} (0.78/3.46); NU-Wave~2 is slightly worse,
0.989/2.57 against 0.94/2.75. We wrote seven predictions before running EARS; four hold.

\section{The Relation Between Dispersion and Level}
Let the truth's high band have energy $E$, the output's $pE$, and let
$c=\mathrm{Re}\langle y_H,\hat y_H\rangle/E$ be the coherent fraction. With a correct low band, the squared
error is $\|y-\hat y\|^2=E\,(1+p-2c)$. An empty band costs $E$, and only $c>p/2$ beats it, so when
$c\approx0$ silence maximizes SNR. On EARS this ceiling $C$ is 21.16\,dB at 12\,kHz; LISA's released L1 loss
(\texttt{det\_paper}, Table~\ref{tab:arms}) comes within 0.19\,dB of it while leaving the band 18.1\,dB
short. A reported SNR $S$ bounds the level: $p\le10^{(C-S)/10}-1+2c$. On its VCTK test split, NU-Wave~2's
own table ($C-S=0.5$\,dB) gives at most $-8.5$\,dB; we measure $-8.9$\,dB there and $-9.1$\,dB on EARS
(broadband; $-4.86$\,dB band-averaged, Table~\ref{tab:released}). Every model has $c\le0.022$.

For $M$ zero-mean draws independent of one another and of the truth, the gap
$G=10\log_{10}\bigl(\overline{\|y-x_i\|^2}/\|y-\bar x\|^2\bigr)$ is, in expectation,
$10\log_{10}\frac{1+p}{1+p/M}$: a function of level alone. A calibrated sampler has $p=1$ and a gap of
2.50\,dB at $M=8$, the spread--skill relation for a reliable ensemble \citep{fortin2014}. We report level
first and judge each model's gap against the independent value at its own level.

\section{Auditing Released Models}
\textbf{FLowHigh is a regressor.} It is trained from $x_0+\epsilon$ ($x_0$: input log-mel) toward
$(x_1-x_0)-(1-\sigma)\epsilon$ and also receives $x_0$ as conditioning, so at $t=0$ it can recover
$\epsilon$. The optimal field then returns $\mathrm{E}[x_1\mid x_0]+\sigma s\epsilon$ after one Euler step,
for any start-noise scale $s$; the released script also resets $s$ to $10^{-4}$, and its eight draws then
agree to $3\times10^{-4}$ (relative RMS, waveform). With the trained prior restored they differ a ninth as
much as NU-Wave~2's above 6\,kHz: the gap is 0.06\,dB, 1.42\,dB [1.30, 1.53] below the 1.48 of independent
draws at its level, and the truth lies below or above all eight draws in 66\% [64, 67] and 23\% [22, 24] of
HB bins.

\textbf{NU-Wave~2 samples, quietly.} Its gap (0.50\,dB) is close to that of independent draws at its level
(0.56), 0.05\,dB [0.05, 0.06] below. What fails is its level: 5.8\,dB under on loud frames and 10.8\,dB over
on quiet ones. Its small spread (0.085) is a level problem, not a diversity one.

\textbf{AudioSR is under-dispersed.} It leads on LSD and ViSQOL, yet its gap (2.37\,dB) is 1.31\,dB
[1.19, 1.44] below that of independent draws at its level (3.69), and the truth lies below or above every
draw in 29\% [28, 30] and 20\% [18, 21] of HB bins. AP-BWE is a point estimate by design.

\textbf{Level regresses per utterance.} The deficit falls with the truth's HB energy share (slope in dB/dB):
$-0.14$ FLowHigh, $-0.20$ AP-BWE, $-0.41$ NU-Wave~2, $-0.27$ AudioSR, $-0.27$ to $-0.41$ our arms. Bright
utterances come out too dark, dark ones too bright.

\section{What Sets the Deficit}
Six arms of an 88k-parameter INR derived from LISA \citep{kim2022} are trained on VCTK on identical batches
for 104{,}950 steps. \texttt{det\_paper} reproduces LISA's released L1 loss; \texttt{det} adds a
log-magnitude term to the same point estimate. The remaining four are samplers, with 8 Gaussian input
channels concatenated with the signal, and 4 per output sample in the decoder-noise arm
(Table~\ref{tab:arms}). \texttt{det\_paper} leaves the band 18.1\,dB short; the log-magnitude term recovers
15.4\,dB, and replacing the point loss by a two-draw energy score \citep{gritsenko2020,bouchacourt2016}
recovers 6.0\,dB more. The loss domain, not point estimation as such, does most of the work: FLowHigh, a
conditional mean in the log-mel domain, is not quiet. On EARS the samplers overshoot: every Table~\ref{tab:released} model but
AudioSR over-fills the top third octave (ours $+11$\,dB at 21\,kHz, AP-BWE $+15$\,dB), so our sampler's
band-averaged deficit is $+3.4$\,dB while its broadband one is $-0.8$\,dB. The ERB-band term lowers the
band-correlation error from 1.09 to 0.97. Our sampler is not calibrated either: its gap is 0.27\,dB
[0.24, 0.29] below independent draws, and the truth lies below all draws in 48\% [47, 49] of HB bins and
above all in 6\%, against an ideal 11\% each; we had predicted the opposite tail.

\section{Conclusion}
Single-reference metrics cannot tell whether a BWE model samples, and with incoherent bands spread must be
read against level. Their rankings also move with the corpus: FLowHigh leads on VCTK, AudioSR on EARS. We
suggest reporting level (per band and frame loudness), diversity relative to the model's own level, and
rank tails beside LSD and ViSQOL. CRPS favours samplers by construction, so we compare it among samplers
only. A low level is a calibration failure for a sampler, not necessarily a perceptual one
\citep{nilsson2001}. Limitations: no listening test, marginal calibration only, six test speakers, one
seed, our network trained on VCTK only; three of our seven predictions failed (repository,
\texttt{supplementary/}).
